Executive summary
This study evaluates the potential for China’s 
adoption of zero-emission heavy-duty trucks 
for regional delivery (RD) and long-haul (LH) 
applications between 2024 and 2035. It examines 
this potential under multiple scenarios, to inform 
zero-emission truck target-setting and policy 
development in China.

HIGHLIGHTS
 ▪ This study evaluates the potential for China’s 
adoption of zero-emission heavy-duty trucks 
for regional delivery (RD) and long-haul (LH) 
applications between 2024 and 2035. 
 ▪ It examines this potential under various 
scenarios, aiming to inform zero-emission truck 
(ZET) target-setting and policy development.
 ▪ The expected availability of a robust charging 
network makes pursuing a high ZET adoption 
target by 2030 possible for RD tractor trailers.
 ▪ For the LH application, a ZET promotion target 
could be considered for 2035. 
 ▪ Current policies are proving effective in 
boosting ZET market share through 2027, 
particularly for battery electric RD tractor 
trailers. However, without sustained policies, 
ZET market share will likely decline afterward. 
 ▪ Cutting road charges by half for ZETs is the 
most impactful policy identified in this study, 
although it could reduce public revenue. 
 ▪ Expanding the charging network is the second-
most influential—but essential—policy measure. 
Charging infrastructure deployment strategies 
should be tailored to the specific needs of RD 
and LH applications. 
 ▪ Both fuel cell electric truck (FCET) purchase 
subsidies and hydrogen refueling subsidies 
are essential through 2030 to support 
continued FCET adoption.
ABOUT THIS REPORT
To reduce carbon and air pollutant emissions, it will be 
important to promote ZETs—battery electric trucks and 
fuel cell electric trucks. 
The adoption of zero-emission heavy-duty trucks (HDTs) 
in China grew rapidly from 2021 to 2024, with zero-
emission HDTs accounting for 14 percent of new HDT 
sales by the end of 2024 (CATARC 2025). Although 
the current adoption of ZETs is primarily concentrated 
in closed-loop applications and urban delivery (Li et al. 
2024a; NEICV 2022),1 zero-emission heavy-duty trucks 
are expected to be used more often for both regional deliv-
ery and long-haul applications—the two most common 
HDT applications in China (CATARC 2017). 
At present, China lacks clear targets, roadmaps, and 
systematic policy incentives to drive broader adoption of 
zero-emission heavy-duty trucks (Chen and Xue 2024; 
Al-Alawi et al. 2022). Clear targets and policies for the 
deployment of zero-emission heavy-duty trucks could 
benefit China in multiple ways. They reduce investment 
risks and attract private capital for ZET production and 
infrastructure development. Also, such targets will help 
China reach national climate goals, yield substantial public 
health benefits through reduced air pollutant emissions, 
and strengthen China’s economy by advancing ZET 
industries (Ghate et al. 2025).
This study aims to provide policymakers with insights into 
how China can accelerate the adoption of zero-emission 
heavy-duty trucks in key applications in the near term 
(by 2035) through demand-side policy interventions. To 
address these questions, the study focuses on regional 
delivery and long-haul applications. Both are widespread 
but face significant challenges transitioning to zero-
emissions. The analysis concentrates on tractor trailers and 
straight trucks, as they are the most commonly used in RD 
and LH operations. Since China’s heavy-duty truck fleet 
is largely operated by small and medium-sized enterprises 
(SMEs) (TUC 2022), this study includes affiliated and 
self-employed individuals, to evaluate ZET adoption from 
the perspective of these smaller fleet operators.
This study assesses the potential for China’s adoption 
of zero-emission heavy-duty trucks between 2024 and 
2035, for the RD and LH applications, under three sets of 
scenarios. The research methodology and modeling steps 
are outlined below and in Figure ES-1.

To assess the future ZET market share by application 
from 2024 to 2035 without policy intervention, this study 
projects ZET payback periods and total cost of owner-
ship (TCO) by application. Two models are then used to 
forecast market share: an adapted version of the predefined 
relationship between ZET payback periods and market 
share, as formulated by the US Environmental Protection 
Agency (EPA 2024a), and TransTEC (ECD of SCUT 
2024), a discrete choice model that estimates ZET market 
share based on TCO. To estimate ZET payback periods 
and TCO, this study identifies the key factors that affect 
the decision of fleet operators (particularly SMEs) to 
switch to ZETs and quantifies these factors in terms of 
payback periods and TCO.2 
To evaluate the impact of different demand-side policy 
interventions on future ZET market share, this study 
constructs two policy scenarios (Table ES-1): one reflect-
ing current policies (such as the trade-in subsidies and 
tax breaks) and the other incorporating additional policy 
incentives aimed at increased ZET adoption (such as 
reducing road charges and accelerating charging infrastruc-
ture deployment). This study also uses uncertainty analysis 
to explore how operational, technological, cost, and policy 
uncertainty affect the ability of policy to drive near-term 
ZET adoption.  
Notably, battery-swapping battery electric trucks (BETs) 
are excluded from this study due to the lack of data.

KEY FINDINGS
We projected zero-emission heavy-duty trucks’ payback 
periods and market share for regional and long-haul appli-
cations between model year (MY)2025 and MY2035. In 
this study, zero-emission heavy-duty trucks are considered 
to be cost-competitive when the payback period is within 
five years for regional delivery, and within four years for 
long-haul transport.
The key findings are as follows:
First, without policy incentives, regional delivery would see 
a modest ZET market share of 2–17 percent by MY2030, 
while ZET adoption would remain at a nascent stage for 
long-haul trucking through MY2035. Battery electric 
trucks will dominate regional delivery, but for long-haul 
application, the optimal technological mix is uncertain.
In both applications, zero-emission tractor trailers are 
more likely to achieve a wide adoption than zero-emission 
straight trucks. This is primarily due to a wider cost gap 
between zero-emission straight trucks and their diesel 
counterparts, driven by greater payload losses in zero-emis-
sion straight trucks. Additionally, diesel straight trucks are 
generally more energy-efficient and have lower purchase 
costs than diesel tractor trailers. 
In terms of technology selection, for regional delivery, bat-
tery electric trucks are more cost-competitive than fuel cell 
electric trucks and are expected to dominate this market 
segment. For long-haul trucking (particularly for tractor 
trailers), the optimal technological mix is uncertain. Spe-
cifically, BETs are more competitive in short daily vehicle 
kilometers traveled (DVKT), low time sensitivity, and no 
payload-loss operations, whereas FCETs are more eco-
nomically viable in long DVKT and high time-sensitivity 
operations with heavy goods transportation.3 
Second, current policies analyzed by this study are 
effective in increasing ZET market share, particularly 
for battery electric tractor trailers in the long annual 
vehicle kilometers traveled (AVKT) operations of the 
RD application (Figure ES-2); however, the near-term 
sustainability of these policies in maintaining ZET 
adoption should be considered. 
Among all the current policies examined in this study, 
the trade-in subsidies are most effective in reducing ZET 
payback periods and increasing market share. By contrast, 
the ZET tax breaks and the FCET City Cluster subsidies 
have a limited impact. 
However, attention should be given to 2028, when the cur-
rent policies, including the trade-in subsidies and ZET tax 
breaks, are set to expire, while the total cost of ownership for 
ZETs remains high. Without sustained policies, ZET mar-
ket share would be expected to decline (see Figure ES-2).
Third, compared to the current policies, the enhanced 
policies analyzed in this study would greatly improve 
ZET market share in regional delivery, while the long-
haul application would require stronger and more 
sustained policy support through MY2035.

Further, the impacts from the enhanced policies vary by 
policy intensity (Figures ES-3 and ES-4). For example, 
if expressway charges were reduced further from 15–50 
percent (Road_charge_50%), Road_charge_50% would 
overtake Accel_charge (accelerated expansion of public 
DCFC stations and lowered overnight charging costs) and 
become the most effective policy analyzed in this study to 
accelerate ZET adoption. 
Fourth, operational, technological, and price uncertain-
ties greatly affect the above conclusions on future ZET 
market share. First, operational variations—such as dif-
ferences in annual vehicle kilometers traveled, daily vehicle 
kilometers traveled, cargo types, and time sensitivity—intro-
duce significant uncertainty in ZET adoption rates. For 
example, for regional delivery, under the No policy scenario, 
ZET market share by MY2030 could range from 1 to 48 
percent due to operational variations. Further, the great 
uncertainties in BET adoption also arise from variations in 
energy efficiency, battery costs, diesel prices, and charging 
costs. For example, under the Current policy scenario, a ±10 
percent change in these parameters would lead to ZET 
market share in MY2027 ranging between 4 and 65 percent. 
POLICY IMPLICATIONS
The above findings also lead to these possible policy  
implications:
First, China’s national governments could establish 
application- and segment-specific ZET promotion tar-
gets. ZET target setting in the five-year plans is important 
to provide predictability for companies along the ZET 
manufacturing supply chain and to guide infrastructure 
investments. Given the variation in ZET market share 
across different applications and truck segments, these 
targets could be differentiated accordingly. 
The RD application holds great potential for near-
term ZET adoption, particularly for the tractor trailer 
segment. A high ZET adoption target could be set for 
this segment by 2030. With a robust charging network 
expected to be in place by MY2030 (the Accel_charge 
scenario), the market share of zero-emission tractor 
trailers in the RD application could reach 33–57 percent 
(Figure ES-5).

By contrast, given that ZET adoption remains at its early 
stage for long-haul transport, a modest promotion target 
is advisable by 2030, but a relatively high target could be 
considered for 2035, particularly for the tractor trailer 
segment. For example, with a well-developed charging 
network (the Accel_charge scenario), zero-emission tractor 
trailers would achieve a 15–53 percent market share in the 
LH application by MY2035. 
Given the significant uncertainties associated with ZET 
adoption resulting from operational, technological, and 
price uncertainties, complementary measures could 
be considered to mitigate volatility and support the 
achievement of ZET promotion targets. These measures 
include reducing fossil fuel subsidies and cracking down 
on illegal and underpriced fuel supplies, strengthen-
ing the supply chain resilience of original equipment 
manufacturers, developing futures markets for critical 
materials and hydrogen, and introducing carbon pric-
ing on transportation fuels (OIES 2024; MPP et al. 
2022; Yang 2020). 
Second, although this study finds a 50 percent reduc-
tion in road charges during the 2024–35 period to be 
the most effective policy for accelerating ZET adoption, 
its implementation should be approached with caution. 
Our analysis shows that 50 percent reductions in road 
charges (Road_charge_50%) during the MY2024–35 
period deliver strong ZET adoption outcomes, compa-
rable to those achieved under a full exemption (Road_
charge_100%). This suggests that a partial reduction in 
road charges would be sufficient.
Notably, this study does not assess the fiscal impact of 
road charge reductions on government revenue. Therefore, 
a comprehensive evaluation is needed to determine the 
viability of such measures. In addition, to mitigate poten-

charging infrastructure policies should not only pri-

a fully built-out public DCFC charging network 
would be essential to support ZET adoption. 
 ▪ Accel_MCS scenario: Achieving 100 percent 
national coverage of both public MCS and DCFC 
stations by 2035. This suggests that even in the 
absence of low-cost overnight charging, a nationwide 
public network of DCFC and MCS stations would 
sufficiently support ZET adoption, provided that 
MCS charging costs remain low. 
Fourth, under this study’s assumptions, both fuel 
cell electric truck purchase subsidies and hydrogen 
subsidies would be useful before 2030 to sustain 
FCET adoption. As the total cost of ownership of 
FCETs declines over time, the need for different types 
of subsidies evolves. In the near term (MY2025–30), 
both FCET purchase subsidies (FCET_purchase) and 
hydrogen refueling subsidies (H2_subsidy) are essential 
for supporting FCET adoption in both regional delivery 
and long-haul applications, except for regions where 
low-cost hydrogen is already available. Over the long 
term (post-MY2030), as FCET purchase prices decline, 
the hydrogen refueling subsidies assumed in this study 
would be sufficient to maintain FCET adoption, par-
ticularly for the long-haul application. 
Fifth, the effectiveness of some ZET incentives 
varies across truck segments and applications. In the 
near term, tractor trailers present stronger oppor-
tunities for ZET adoption, while zero-emission 
straight trucks would need greater policy support.  
For example, in the regional delivery application, battery 
electric tractor trailers benefit more from current policies 
than battery electric straight trucks due to smaller cost 
differences with their diesel counterparts, compared to 
battery electric straight trucks. As such, prioritizing ZET 
adoption in the tractor trailer segment is more feasible 
in the short term. In contrast, promoting zero-emission 
straight trucks would require additional policy sup-
port. For example, ZET weight allowance is especially 
crucial for battery electric straight trucks, due to greater 
payload losses associated with battery electric straight 
trucks. Likewise, fuel-cell (FC) straight trucks would 
need higher subsidies than FC tractor trailers, given their 
larger purchase cost gap relative to diesel counterparts. 
Lastly, statistics on truck operational profiles by 
application and truck segment need to be improved.  
Data on the mileage and load profiles of existing heavy-
duty truck fleets and truck sales by application are 
essential for effective policymaking and industrial col-
laboration. In China, to prevent drivers from speeding 
or working overtime, the measures articulated in MOT 
et al. 2014 require all heavy-duty vehicles to install the 
global positioning system, which has provided a viable 
way to collect HDTs’ mileage profiles. To support the 
further adoption of ZET trucks, it is important for 
national governments and relevant agencies to gather 
and analyze these data by application and truck seg-
ment, and share the findings with key stakeholders, such 
as other government ministries and private sector actors 
(like charging point operators).

China currently lacks clear targets and

Heavy-duty trucks (HDTs) with a gross vehicle weight 
rating (GVWR) of over 12 tons are used for a wide variety 
of applications. Although they account for 3 percent of 
vehicles on the road in China, they contributed 35 percent 
of China’s road transport CO2 emissions, 76 percent of 
NOx, and 50 percent of particulate matter (PM) emis-
sions in 2023 (MEE 2025; CATARC 2025; Xue and Liu 
2022).4 Zero-emission trucks (ZETs)—including battery 
electric trucks (BETs) and hydrogen fuel cell electric trucks 
(FCETs)—are key solutions for mitigating climate change 
and improving local air quality.
Adoption of zero-emission HDTs grew rapidly in China 
during the 2021–24 period due to declining battery costs 
(TrendForce 2025) and the introduction of trade-in 
subsidies (MOT et al. 2025; MOT and MOF 2024).5 As 
a result, zero-emission HDTs accounted for 14 per-
cent of new HDT sales by the end of 2024 (CATARC 
2025; Figure 1). 
The current adoption of zero-emission HDTs is primarily 
concentrated in closed-loop applications (such as HDTs 
operating within factories or ports) and urban delivery 
(Li et al. 2024a; NEICV 2022). However, zero-emission 
HDTs are expected to expand into the regional delivery 
and long-haul applications, which are the two most 
common heavy-duty truck applications in China (CAT-
ARC 2017). This transition, particularly in the long-haul 
segment, presents major challenges due to technological 
limitations, high costs, and inadequate infrastructure 
(Nykvist and Olsson 2021).

investment uncertainty, attract private capital for

that climate goals are met, improve public health by 
reducing air pollution, and enhance the country’s 
economic competitiveness (Ghate et al. 2025).

and targeted policies—such as infrastructure

support widescale deployment. Once clear

expanded to close the existing policy gaps and ensure

The research methodology and modeling steps are outlined

To assess the future ZET market share by application from 
2024–35 without policy intervention, this study projects 
ZET payback periods and total cost of ownership (TCO) 
by application. These projections are then used in two 
models—the adapted EPA (2024a) relationship and Trans-

study identifies the key factors that affect the deci-

periods and TCOs.

study constructs two policy scenarios: one reflecting cur-

incentives aimed at accelerating ZET adoption. This study 
also explores how operational, technological, cost, and

ZET adoption through uncertainty analysis.

DEFINITION OF 
APPLICATIONS AND ZET 
CONFIGURATION
The study first defines the RD and LH applications and 
then configurates ZETs’ specifications (including battery 
capacities and fuel cell power ratings) to meet the opera-
tional requirements of each application. 
In this study, RD and LH applications are defined based 
on DVKT , using 800 kilometers (km) as the threshold—
the most used cutoff. There is no standardized classification 
for RD and LH applications in China. For example, one 
study (Niu et al. 2023) classified the LH application as a 
DVKT above 500 km, whereas others (China SAE n.d.; 
Pihlatie et al. 2023) define it as above 800 km or 1,000 km. 
This study adopts 800 km DVKT as the threshold between 
the RD and LH applications because it is a relatively 
common cutoff (Pers. Comm. 2024; Pihlatie et al. 2023; 
Jiang et al. 2022). This definition also captures various 
LH subcategories, such as return-to-base operations and 
over-the-road operations (with long DVKT and infrequent 
returns to home base) (Roeth 2025).  
The DVKT and AVKT for each application are presented 
in Table 1. These values are derived from Pers. Comm. 
2024 and a literature review (Table 1), while considering 
the correlation between DVKT and AVKT (Goeckeler 
et al. 2023). To capture the operational variations of 
SMEs, we analyzed a range of AVKT (including short 
and long AVKT operations) in each application. Further, 
we conducted a sensitivity analysis to assess the impact of 
different DVKT on the results. 
This study assumes that AVKT will not degrade by age 
(Pers. Comm. 2024), and that ZETs and diesel trucks will 
have the same AVKT and useful years.
This study adopts different methods and assumptions to 
configurate BETs and FCETs. 
For FCETs, their energy storage capacities are determined 
based on DVKT , and we assume no infrastructure con-
straints for FCETs. 
For BETs, battery capacities and charging powers are 
configurated in multiple ways based on travel distances and 
the availability of different types of charging stations (see 
Figure 5). These configurations allow for trade-offs among 
key factors influencing BET adoption, such as purchase 
costs, payload losses, and range anxiety.

SELECTION AND 
QUANTIFICATION OF 
DECISION VARIABLES 
The trucking industry is a highly market-driven sector, 
where fleet operators—especially SMEs—play a critical 
role in determining whether to transition to ZETs. Their 
decision-making is affected not only by cost-related factors 
(such as vehicle purchase costs) but also by factors not 
related to cost, such as range anxiety, ZET payload losses, 
risk aversion to new technologies, and environmental 
awareness (Hunter et al. 2021; Burnham et al. 2021). 
This study quantifies 11 key decision variables that greatly 
influence fleet operators’ purchase decisions regarding 
ZETs. Notably, unlike existing studies on estimation of 
China ZETs’ TCO (CATARC 2022; Mao et al. 2021), 
this study monetizes three variables not related to cost, 
including range anxiety, payload losses, and charging time 
penalty (see “Quantification of charging time penalty . . 
.”). However, some variables not related to cost—such as 
environmental consciousness, vehicle safety concerns, tech-
nology familiarity, and ZET model availability—remain 
difficult to quantify and are therefore excluded. 
Beyond these decision variables, policy regulations also 
impact fleet operators’ ZET adoption decisions. Instead of 
assessing policy impacts as a separate variable, this study

QUANTIFICATION OF 
CHARGING TIME PENALTY, 
RANGE ANXIETY, AND 
RESIDUAL VALUES 
Charging time penalty
“Charging time penalty” refers to the additional time 
required for daytime opportunity charging compared to 
the refueling time of a diesel truck. In this study, the pen-
alty applies exclusively to BET charging during daytime 
hours. Overnight charging for BETs and overnight refuel-
ing for FCETs are not considered, since these activities are 
assumed not to interfere with truck operation. 
Further, only daytime opportunity charging sessions incur 
a penalty, while overnight charging is assumed to be less 
time-constrained and therefore subject to no penalties. 
However, this assumption may not hold for multishift 
operations in the LH application and could lead to an 
underestimation of the time penalty in that application. 
In this study, the charging time penalty is calculated 
by multiplying the daytime charging duration by 
a value of time.
 ▪  The value of time can be derived from driver labor 
costs (Burnham et al. 2021) or cargo detention 
rates (Hunter et al. 2021). This study adopts cargo 
detention rates because they are higher than labor 
costs and can have more tangible impacts on BET 
costs. Based on a literature review (Truck Alliance 
2025; MOT and NDRC 1998), the value of time 
(detention rate) is assumed to be 50 percent of the 
hourly freight rate, that is, 150 CNY/hour. For 
sensitivity analysis, the value of time is assumed to 
be 100 percent of the hourly freight rate—300 CNY/
hour based on a literature review (Ledna et al. 2024; 
Hunter et al. 2021). 
 ▪  For simplicity, this study only considers the daytime 
charging duration and excludes the additional time 
needed to locate and wait for charging infrastructure.
Range anxiety
Range anxiety—a major barrier to ZET adoption—is the 
concern that a vehicle may run out of electricity before 
reaching a charging station (Hao et al. 2022; Neubauer 
and Wood 2014). The severity of range anxiety depends 
on the availability of public truck charging infrastructure. 
This study assumes that range anxiety applies only to 
shorter-range BETs, while longer-range BETs or FCETs 
are not affected, given the assumption of no infrastructure 
constraints on depots or NCS.
In this study, range anxiety is quantified as the additional 
operational costs incurred when truck drivers use alterna-
tive diesel trucks to complete trips that shorter-range 
BETs cannot fulfill (Hao et al. 2022). The cost is calculated 
following Equation 1, where the likelihood of using an

dong Province and the Beijing-Tianjin-Hebei region, 
and gradually extend nationwide. This assumed expansion 
in the No policy scenario is based on a literature review 
(Zhao et al. 2023; China SAE 2024; Zhang et al. 2021).

Residual values
In well-developed market conditions, ZET residual val-
ues will be much higher than in the No policy scenario. 
Under such conditions, assuming the glider will retain 30 
percent of its value, while battery packs and fuel cell sys-
tems will retain 43 percent and 50 percent, respectively, 
after their first use (Basma and Rodríguez 2023), the 
overall residual value of ZETs would be 20–38 percent of 
their retail price.
However, driven by battery degradation risks, rapid tech-
nological advances, and an underdeveloped secondhand 
market, ZETs cannot realize their full market value upon 
resale (BNEF 2024). Therefore, this study assumes that, 
under the No Policy scenario, ZETs will retain the same 
residual value as diesel trucks in absolute terms. Diesel 
trucks are assumed to retain about 30 percent of their 
retail price as residual value after first use (Pers. Comm. 
2024; Basma et al. 2021). 
The above assumption likely underestimates ZET 
residual values (and their cost-competitiveness). Under 
the above assumption, ZET residual values account for 
7–25 percent of their retail price, with particularly low 
residual values for longer-range BETs in the LH applica-
tion, where larger battery capacity would typically result 
in higher resale value. To remedy this problem, we also 
developed several scenarios with increased ZET residual 
values to evaluate their impact on ZET adoption (see 
the subsection below “Scenario analysis and uncer-
tainty analysis”).

▪  The US EPA’s (2024a) ZET adoption trajectory

which sets ZET market share caps at 20 percent by 
MY2027, 37 percent by MY2030, and 70 percent by

2024; China SAE 2024) and the TransTEC model. 
Given China’s more advanced supply chain and rapid 
charging infrastructure deployment, higher ZET 
market share caps are applied: 7–85 percent for the 
RD application during the MY2027–30 period, 
and 70–85 percent for the LH application during 
the MY2030–35 period. These China-specific ZET 
market share caps are supported by recent ZET 
adoption trends (CATARC 2025) and research (Ke 
et al. 2024; China SAE 2024).

by simulating consumers’ choice probabilities across 
different powertrains and ZET configurations 
(ECD of SCUT 2024). The model adopts a standard 
consumers’ choice probability modeling framework 
(Hao et al. 2024; Ou et al. 2022; Liu et al. 2020; Lin 
and Greene 2010), where the choice probabilities

this study does not distinguish between market 
segments (such as fleet operators by company size); 
all fleet operators in China are treated as a single, 
unified segment. Further, model endogeneity—such 
as the feedback between cumulative ZET sales 
and battery cost reductions—is not considered for 
simplicity. Given the uncertainties in operation, 
technological advances, costs, and policy intensity, 
this study provides a range of projected ZET market 
shares rather than a single forecast.

the adapted EPA relationship is used to project ZET 
market share for milestone years (including 2030, 2032, 
and 2035), while the TransTEC model is used to extrap-

SCENARIO ANALYSIS AND 
UNCERTAINTY ANALYSIS 
Scenario analysis: This study uses a forward simulation 
method to design and forecast policy scenarios, where 
each scenario is implemented with specific policies 
without setting predetermined targets for ZET market 
share. Given data limitations, this study only investigates 
the impact of proposed policies on ZET payback periods 
(specifically, whether they fall below four to five years) 
and market share. We do not quantify government expen-
ditures, or the broader social costs associated with ZET 
promotion resulting from these policies.
Three groups of scenarios are constructed to forecast 
ZET market share (Table 4):
 ▪ No policy scenario: No policy incentives 
are considered. 
 ▪ Current policy scenario: These scenarios model 
existing national-level policies—issued or 
planned—individually and collectively. This includes 
trade-in subsidies, tax breaks, and FCET City 
Cluster subsidies. Due to modeling limitations, 
other influential demand-side policies such as the 
Performance Rating Scheme (MEE 2020) are 
not considered. 9 
Further, as there are currently no national policies 
specifically promoting public charging infrastructure 
for trucks, the availability of public truck charging 
infrastructure is assumed to be the same as in 
the No policy scenario. This assumption may 
underestimate charging infrastructure availability in 
the RD application. 
 ▪ Enhanced policy scenarios: These scenarios build 
on the Current policy scenario to evaluate the 
effectiveness of additional (demand-side) policies 
for ZET adoption. In practice, demand-side 
policy incentives can generally be grouped into the 
categories of financial incentives, infrastructure 
safeguards, and regulations (OIES n.d.; WEF et al. 
2021). Not all potential demand-side policies are 
included in this study. We focus on demand-side 
policies that are representative of each category and 
effective in promoting ZET adoption, are currently 
being considered by the Chinese government, and 
can be simulated using our model. However, policies 
like the Performance Rating Scheme and carbon 
pricing are excluded, since they either require more 
sophisticated models or would have limited effects 
(Chen and Xue 2024; Mao et al. 2021). Based on 
the criteria, we chose the five policy scenarios that 
appear in blue in Table 4. These were classified in 
three categories: 
 ▪ Infrastructure safeguard: accelerated charging 
network expansion
 ▪ Financial incentives: road charge reductions, FCET 
subsidies, residual value guarantee 
 ▪ Regulation: ZET weight allowance

Uncertainty analysis: Given high uncertainty surround-
ing technological progress, fuel prices, truck operational 
profiles, and policies, the study also conducts a sensitivity 
analysis for each scenario to assess how the conclusions 
might change with variations in key assumptions. To 
reduce the combinations, we limited the scope of the 
sensitivity analysis. 
 ▪ This study first examines how ZET adoption varies 
by operational uncertainty in the No policy scenario. 
The operational uncertainty is characterized by 
variations in AVKT , DVKT , time sensitivity of 
transportation (that is, higher value of time for 
charging time penalty), and type of cargo transported 
(payload losses). The degree of operational variation 
is based on a review of China-specific literature 
(Hao et al. 2024; China SAE 2024; CATARC 2022, 
2018; China SAE et al. 2016) and Pers. Comm. 
(2024) (Table 1). 
 ▪ This study further evaluates how technological, 
operational, and cost uncertainties affect ZET 
adoption in the Current policy scenario. Nearly all 
the key variables in this study (18 parameters) are 
considered. The degree of variation follows a typical

This section projected ZETs’ payback periods and 
market share without any policy incentives across 
the regional delivery and long-haul applications. The

ZETS INCREASE COST-
COMPETITIVENESS IN 
REGIONAL DELIVERY BY 
MY2030–34, BUT LONG-
HAUL ADOPTION REMAINS 
NASCENT
We projected ZETs’ payback periods and market share 
without any ZET policy incentives across the RD and LH 
applications from MY2025 to MY2035. The objective is 
to analyze when the payback periods of ZETs would fall 
within five years for the RD application and four years for 
the LH application—thresholds at which fleet owners may 
be more inclined to purchase ZETs—and to estimate the 
corresponding ZET market share.
The results show that without policy interventions, the 
greatest ZET promotion opportunity in the near term (by 
2030) comes from battery electric tractor trailers operating 
in the RD application, while the ZET adoption remains at 
the early stage in the LH application.
In the RD application, battery electric tractor trailers are 
expected to be more cost-competitive by MY2030–34, 
with a market penetration of 2–17 percent in MY2030. 
In the RD application, BETs are more cost-competitive 
than FCETs. This is because FCETs still have high pur-
chase costs and limited operational savings (Appendix C). 
Among the battery electric powertrains, BET200_MCS 
are less economical than BET200_DCFC and BET400 
due to higher charging costs, which offset MCS’ benefits 
from a reduced charging time penalty; and greater range 
anxiety, caused by the limited availability of MCS infra-
structure (Appendix C). 
In the RD application, battery electric tractor trailers are 
more cost-competitive than battery electric straight trucks. 
 ▪ Battery electric tractor trailers are projected to be 
cost-competitive around MY2030–34, depending on 
AVKT . In long AVKT (100,000 km), the five-year 
payback period of battery electric tractor trailers will 
be achieved by MY2030, while in short AVKT (60,000 
km), the five-year-payback period will be reached much 
later—around MY2034 (Figure 8). Further, longer-
range BETs (BET400) will be more economical before 
MY2030. However, as public DCFC charging stations 
expand, shorter-range BETs (BET200) will become 
more competitive after MY2030. 
 ▪ Battery electric straight trucks will become 
economically viable between MY2032 and MY2035, 
later than battery electric tractor trailers. In long 
AVKT , the five-year payback period of battery electric 
straight trucks will occur in MY2032 (two years later 
than tractor trailers), while in short AVKT , the five-year 
payback period will be reached by MY2035 (one year 
later than tractor trailers) (Figure 8). This is due to the 
greater payload losses of battery electric straight trucks 
(Appendix D), better energy efficiency, and lower 
purchase costs of diesel straight trucks compared to 
diesel tractor trailers.

CONSIDERABLE 
UNCERTAINTY EXISTS 
REGARDING ZET 
MARKET SHARE DUE TO 
OPERATIONAL VARIATIONS 
Given the operational variations in the RD and LH 
applications, we analyzed the sensitivity of ZET payback 
periods and market share to different operational factors, 
including AVKT , DVKT , types of cargo transported, and 
levels of time sensitivity.
Since zero-emission tractor trailers are more cost-com-
petitive, we use them as an example to illustrate how the 
above findings change with the operational patterns. 
The results indicate that considerable uncertainty exists 
regarding ZET market share as a result of operational 
variations. The potential for adopting ZETs varies signifi-
cantly within each application, and policymakers and fleet 
operators should identify subapplications with the greatest 
near-term opportunities for ZET adoption. 
In the RD application, the near-term opportunity of 
ZET adoption lies in BETs running in longer AVKT 
and shorter DVKT with limited time sensitivity.  
Our analysis shows that in the RD application, AVKT , 
DVKT , and values of time have strong influence on the 
payback periods and market share of zero-emission tractor 
trailers (see Figure 12). Regardless of operational profiles, 
the battery electric powertrain consistently remains more 
cost-competitive than the FC powertrain:
 ▪ With longer AVKT , payback periods for all zero-
emission powertrains will be shortened due to increased 
operational savings. 
 ▪ With shorter DVKT (from 400 to 300 km),11 
payback periods for all zero-emission powertrains 
will improve significantly. This improvement is driven 
by lower capital costs (resulting from reduced range 
requirements) for ZETs and decreased daytime 
charging penalties for shorter-range BETs. 
 ▪ With no payload loss, the payback period of zero-
emission tractor trailers improves marginally. In theory, 
this scenario would benefit longer-range BETs, as they 
typically have greater payload losses. However, in the 
RD application, BET400s’ payload losses are limited 
(see Appendix D). As a result, the payback period of 
BET400s does not change significantly.12 
 ▪ A high value of time negatively impacts the payback of 
shorter-range BETs (BET200) because of significant 
penalties associated with daytime opportunity charging. 
However, this scenario benefits longer-range BETs and 
FCETs, as their charging or refueling takes place at 
night when no time penalty applies.

This section assesses the extent to which the enhanced 
policies examined in this study affect ZETs’ payback 
periods and market share. This analysis shows that, while 
enhanced incentives substantially boost ZET uptake in 
the RD application, the LH application demands longer-
lasting and more targeted policy incentives to achieve 
comparable gains.
In the RD application, under the enhanced policies, 
ZET market share would exceed 50 percent between 
MY2027 and MY2030, with the effectiveness of different 
policies varying by truck segment and powertrain. 
Under the combined influence of current and enhanced 
policies, the market share of zero-emission tractor trailers 
and straight trucks in the RD application could surpass 
50 percent between MY2027 and MY2030. However, as 
the current policies phase out after MY2027, ZET growth 
would slow down. For example, in long AVKT operation, 
the market share of zero-emission tractor trailers remains 
nearly unchanged from MY2027 to MY2030. 
Further, the effectiveness of different enhanced policies on 
ZET market share varies by truck segment and zero-
emission powertrain. This underscores the importance of 
targeted policies tailored to specific truck segments and 
powertrains to accelerate ZET adoption.
First, the impact of different enhanced policies varies by 
truck segment. For zero-emission tractor trailers, all five 
enhanced policies—except for Residual_value_20%—play 
an important role, while for zero-emission straight trucks, 
Accel_charge and Weight_increase are more effective. 
For zero-emission tractor trailers, all five enhanced poli-
cies—except for Residual_value_20%—play a significant 
role in boosting ZET market share (Figure 20).
 ▪ Accel_charge (that is, investments in charging 
infrastructure and reductions in overnight charging 
costs) is the most impactful measure in the RD 
application. With this policy, the market share of 
zero-emission tractor trailers would reach 33–65 
percent between MY2027 and MY2030 in both long 
and short AVKT operations, surpassing that of diesel 
trucks. This underscores the critical role of charging 
infrastructure in driving BET adoption.
 ▪ Other enhanced policies—including Road_
charge_15%, Weight_increase, and FCET_
subsidies—are less effective and primarily affect 
long AVKT operations. With these policies working 
in isolation, ZET market share would reach 33–65 
percent by MY2027–30 in long AVKT . However, in 
short AVKT operations, ZET market share would 
remain below 10 percent by MY2030. Of the three 
policies, FCET_subsidies and Road_charge_15% 
are more effective, potentially pushing ZET market 
share above 50 percent, in long AVKT operations. 
 ▪ Residual_value_20% has minimal impact on ZET 
adoption, as it does not sufficiently bridge the cost 
gap between ZETs and diesel trucks. 
For zero-emission straight trucks, Accel_charge and 
Weight_increase are more effective, particularly for BETs 
(Figure 21). Although Accel_charge remains the most 
effective policy for promoting battery electric straight 
trucks, Weight_increase also plays a crucial role due to 
the large payload losses associated with these trucks. Both 
policies are also most effective in long AVKT operations: 
under Accel_charge, ZET market share would reach

The results indicate that when changing the policy design,

Road charge reduction
With 50 percent (Road_charge_50%) and 100 percent 
(Road_charge_100%) reductions in road charges during

The effectiveness of the above policies is subject to four 
additional uncertainties.
First, the impact of road charge reductions may be limited 
in certain cases. For example, Chinese local governments 
provide “green channels”—100 percent expressway toll 
exemptions—to specific diesel trucks, such as those trans-
porting fresh agricultural products. Further, many diesel 
trucks avoid toll roads by using national highways. In both 
cases, road charge reductions would provide limited (or 
no) incentives for ZET adoption; other policy alternatives 
should be sought. 
Second, reducing depot charging costs to 0.6 CNY/kWh 
(Accel_depot) may be difficult in certain operations. For 
example, in over-the-road LH operations, truck drivers 
often cannot return to the depot regularly and must rely 
on public DCFC chargers, which typically have higher 
costs. Also, in time-sensitive operations that often involve 
two drivers sharing the same truck during a 24-hour 
period, truck drivers may be unable to charge overnight 
at depots or through NCS, and instead must rely on day-
time charging, which typically incurs higher costs. This 
may make the Accel_depot scenario difficult to achieve in 
some LH applications.  
Third, the effectiveness of Residual_value_20% or 
(Residual_value_30%) may be overestimated. In the No 
policy scenario, ZET residual values are conservatively set 
at the same low level as diesel trucks. Further, with a robust 
secondhand market for ZETs, a residual value guarantee 
policy would become unnecessary, as resale values would 
naturally reflect market demand. In such cases, ZET 
market values would resemble those projected in the 
Residual_value_30% scenario. Also, to tackle the problem 
of low residual values, other measures can be considered, 
such as extended warranties or financial leasing of ZETs. 
Fourth, FCET_subsidies may not be necessary in regions 
with access to low-cost hydrogen. For example, as esti-
mated by this study, in the LH application, regions with 
already low-cost hydrogen supplies (below 25 CNY/kg) 
would require no additional hydrogen refueling subsidies.

UNCERTAINTY WITH THE 
MODELING METHOD 
Although the methodology of this study is univer-
sally applicable, several uncertainties may affect the 
research conclusions.
First, this study would face the challenges of not account-
ing for factors unrelated to cost that also affect ZET 
adoption, such as revenue gains from transitioning to 
ZETs, safety concerns, model diversity, risk aversion to 
new technologies, and regional disparity. The omission 
of these factors may lead to either an underestimation or 
overestimation of ZET market share. For example, even 
when ZETs offer favorable TCO, payload capacity, and 
other advantages, ZET uptake may still be slow. 
Additionally, the assumption of universal FCET infra-
structure availability in this study tends to favor FCETs. 
However, this assumption is overly optimistic. In reality, 
limited infrastructure availability and range anxiety remain 
major barriers to FCET deployment (Xue et al. 2023).  
Second, fleet operators may place different weights on the 
decision variables. For example, Zhao et al. (2024) sug-
gest that ZET purchase costs are weighted more heavily 
than other factors. If purchase costs were given twice the 
weight of other variables, it would be difficult to achieve 
five-year payback periods for battery electric tractor trailers 
by MY2030 even in long AVKT (their payback periods 
would be above 10 years), and ZET market share in the 
RD application would be much lower than estimated 
in this study. 
Third, given the inherent uncertainties in ZET market 
share forecasting models (EPA 2024b), the projected 
ZET market share by application is also subject to 
variability. However, this study incorporates extensive 
uncertainty analyses, generating a range of market share 
estimates, which helps mitigate potential inaccuracies in 
the projections.
Fourth, due to the lack of comprehensive data on truck 
mileage distribution by application in China, this study 
presents a wide range of ZET market shares under varying 
AVKT and DVKT conditions. To reduce the uncertainties 
in future market share estimates and support more effec-
tive policymaking, we recommend that relevant govern-
ment departments collect and analyze truck mileage data 
disaggregated by application. 
Fifth, the absence of fiscal impact analysis weakens our 
ability to draw more practical policy recommendations. 
This study focuses solely on the effects of ZET adoption 
and, due to data constraints, does not assess the fiscal 
implications of policies such as road charge reductions 
or charging infrastructure deployment. To support more 
informed and cost-effective policymaking, we recommend 
that relevant government departments collect detailed 
truck sales and mileage data by application, enabling more 
accurate projections of overall ZET market share and 
associated fiscal impacts. 
Lastly, evaluating ZET adoption in different geographical 
locations, such as low-temperature environments or hilly 
terrains, would enhance the study’s geographic applicability 
and provide a more comprehensive understanding of ZET 
adoption nationwide.

DEVIATIONS FROM THE 
PREVIOUS STUDY 
This report builds on our earlier study (Chen and Xue 
2024) but differs from it in the following aspects:
First, while both studies indicate that without policy 
incentives, ZETs in RD applications will achieve payback 
periods of five to six years around MY2030–34, the under-
lying assumptions vary. This study assumes lower diesel 
prices and shorter AVKT for the RD application, which 
pose disadvantages for ZET adoption. However, these 
challenges are offset by the lower battery and charging 
costs assumed in this study (see Appendix B). As a result, 
despite differences in assumptions, both studies reach 
similar conclusions regarding the timing of ZET adoption 
in the RD application.
Second, our earlier study (Chen and Xue 2024) found that 
without policy incentives, shorter-range battery electric 
tractor trailers in the port drayage application are more 
cost-competitive than longer-range BETs (reaching six-
year payback periods by MY2022). However, this study 
does not replicate that conclusion. This is because this 
study accounts for the costs associated with range anxiety 
and charging time penalties that make shorter-range BETs 
less favorable than their longer-range counterparts. 
However, if we focus on key regions in China with well-
developed charging infrastructure (that is, where range 
anxiety is no longer a concern), the conclusion will match 
with that of our earlier study (Chen and Xue 2024): 
shorter-range battery electric tractor trailers would achieve 
a five-year payback period by MY2024 in the AVKT case 
of the RD application.  
Third, our earlier study indicated that FCETs are more 
cost-competitive than BETs in the RD application (Chen 
and Xue 2024), whereas this report reaches the opposite 
conclusion. The key difference lies in the assumptions: 
this study assumes lower battery costs and charging costs, 
along with higher hydrogen prices, which favor BETs 
(see Appendix B).

CHAPTER 7.
  
Recommendations 
This study recommends that China’s national 
government set application-specific ZET promotion 
targets and implement a series of proactive 
measures to achieve them.

This report evaluates the near-term ZET adop-
tion potential—including BETs and FCETs— from 
MY2024 to MY2035 for RD and LH applications in 
China. The key findings have the following possible 
policy implications:
First, China’s national government needs to establish 
application-specific ZET promotion targets. ZET 
target setting in the five-year plans is important to provide 
predictability for companies along the ZET manufactur-
ing supply chain and to guide infrastructure investments 
(Ghate et al. 2025). Given the variation in ZET market 
share across different applications, these targets should be 
differentiated accordingly. 
The RD application holds great potential for near-term 
ZET adoption, particularly for the tractor trailer seg-
ment. A high ZET adoption target could be set for this 
segment by 2030. If a well-developed charging network 
is established (the Accel_charge scenario), the market 
share of zero-emission tractor trailers could reach 33–57 
percent by MY2030. 
By contrast, given that ZET adoption remains at its early 
stage in the LH application, a modest promotion target 
is advisable by 2030, but a relatively high target could be 
considered for 2035, particularly for the tractor trailer 
segment. For example, with a well-developed charging 
network (the Accel_charge scenario), zero-emission tractor 
trailers would achieve a 15–53 percent market share in the 
LH application by MY2035. 
Given the high uncertainties associated with ZET 
adoption resulting from operational, technological, and 
price uncertainties, complementary measures should be 
considered to mitigate volatility and support the achieve-
ment of ZET promotion targets. These measures include 
reducing fossil fuel subsidies,  cracking down on illegal and 
underpriced fuel supplies, strengthening the supply chain 
resilience of original equipment manufacturers, developing 
futures markets for critical materials and hydrogen, and 
introducing carbon pricing for transportation fuels (OIES 
2024; MPP et al. 2022; Yang 2020). 
Second, although a 50 percent reduction in road charges 
during the 2024–35 period is the most effective policy 
in this study for accelerating the adoption of ZETs, its 
implementation should be pursued with caution. Since 
Road_charge_50% delivers effects similar to Road_
charge_100% for certain MYs, it is necessary to determine 
an appropriate level of road charge reduction to avoid 
excessive incentives. 
Road_charge_50% is also the most influential policy 
for both BETs and FCETs in the LH application; 
however, its effect on FCETs in the RD application 
is less than that of FCET_subsidies (the combina-
tion of purchase and hydrogen refueling subsidies). 
This suggests that, to accelerate FCET adoption in 
the RD application before MY2030, FCET_subsidies 
assumed in this study are most effective and, for Road_
charge_50% to be effective, it should be paired with 
complementary measures, such as low-cost hydrogen 
or FCET purchase subsidies.
Notably, this study does not assess the fiscal impact of 
road charge reductions on public revenue. Therefore, a 
comprehensive evaluation is still needed to determine 
the viability of such measures. In addition, to mitigate 
potential revenue losses, road charge policies should be 
carefully designed—potentially by imposing higher rates 
on diesel trucks (EU 2022).   
Third, charging network expansion and lowered over-
night charging costs are the second-most influential—
but essential—measures. Further, optimal charging 
station deployment strategies vary by application.
In the RD application, the combined effect (Accel_
charge) of accelerated expansion of public DCFC 
stations (Accel_DCFC, achieving 100 percent national 
coverage of DCFC stations by 2035) and lowered over-
night charging costs (Accel_depot, with 0.6CNY/kWh 
depot/NCS charging cost during the 2024–35 period) 
is the largest. Therefore, charging infrastructure policies 
should not only prioritize the expansion of public truck 
charging stations but also ensure the availability of low-
cost overnight charging at depots or NCS. 
In the LH application, two deployment strate-
gies for charging stations could achieve similar ZET 
adoption outcomes:
 ▪ 100 percent national coverage of public DCFC 
stations by 2035 and lowered overnight charging 
costs at depots or NCS during the 2024–35 period 
(the Accel_charge scenario). This suggests that, 
without the deployment of MCS charging stations, 
a combination of low-cost overnight charging 
and a comprehensive public DCFC charging 
network is necessary. 
 ▪ 100 percent national coverage of both public MCS and 
DCFC stations by 2035 (the Accel_MCS scenario). 
This suggests that, in the absence of low-cost overnight 
charging at depots or NCS, a comprehensive public

network of DCFC and MCS stations would be 
sufficient to drive ZET adoption, provided that MCS 
charging costs are low. 
Further, in the RD application, both shorter- and 
longer-range BETs will capture similar market shares 
by MY2030. By contrast, MCS charging demonstrates 
limited cost-competitiveness, due to high charg-
ing costs and limited availability of MCS chargers 
before MY2030. 
However, in the LH application, the optimal BET con-
figurations differ: under the Accel_charge scenario, both 
longer-range BETs and shorter-range BETs will capture 
similar market shares, while under the Accel_MCS 
scenario, shorter-range BETs will dominate the market. 
This suggests that, for the LH application, the choice of 
optimal BET configurations should be aligned with the 
planned charging infrastructure.
Fourth, under this study’s assumptions, both FCET 
purchase subsidies and hydrogen subsidies are necessary 
before 2030 to maintain future FCET adoption. As FCET 
TCOs decline, the need for various FCET subsidies 
changes over time. In the near term (MY2025–30), both 
FCET purchase subsidies (FCET_purchase) and hydro-

RD and LH applications, except for regions with low-cost 
hydrogen supplies. Over the long term (post-MY2030), as 
FCET purchase costs decline, hydrogen refueling subsidies 
assumed in this study will be sufficient to support FCET 
adoption, particularly in the LH application.
Fifth, some ZET incentives have varying impacts across 
truck segments (and applications); overall, more policy 
supports are needed for zero-emission straight trucks. By 
aligning incentives with application-specific challenges, poli-
cymakers can enhance the effectiveness of ZET incentives. 
For example, FCET subsidy levels need to be adjusted by 
truck segment. FC straight trucks require higher subsidies 
than FC tractor trailers, given their larger purchase cost gap 
with diesel counterparts. Similarly, ZET weight allowance 
is crucial for battery electric straight trucks, due to greater 
payload losses associated with battery electric straight trucks. 
Further, in the RD application, due to smaller cost dif-
ferences with their diesel counterparts, battery electric 
tractor trailers benefit more from current policies than 
battery electric straight trucks. Therefore, near-term 
policy support could prioritize this truck segment to 
maximize ZET adoption. 
Lastly, improved statistics on truck operational profiles by 
application and truck segment are necessary. Data on the 
mileage and load profiles of existing heavy-duty truck fleets 
and truck sales by application are essential for effective poli-
cymaking and industrial collaboration. In China, to prevent 
drivers from speeding or working overtime, the measures 
articulated in MOT et al. 2014 require all heavy-duty 
vehicles to install GPS, which provides a viable way to col-
lect HDTs’ mileage profiles. To support the further adoption 
of ZET trucks, it is important for the national government 
and relevant agencies to gather and analyze these data by 
application and truck segment, and share the findings with 
key stakeholders, such as other government ministries and 
private sector actors (like charging point operators).

Energy costs
Energy costs are estimated by multiplying energy efficiency 
with energy prices. In this study, we calculated energy prices 
(including diesel prices, charging costs, and hydrogen prices) 
as detailed below. 
Diesel prices
Diesel price for 2024 is averaged at 7.5 CNY/L (Eastmoney 
2022). Future diesel prices are projected based on the aver -
age from the past five years (2020–24) and set at 7.0 CNY/L 
(Eastmoney 2022). 17 This price remains constant for projecting 
diesel truck costs from 2025 and 2035. The sensitivity of diesel 
prices on the ZET adoption is discussed above in the subsec -
tion “Considerable uncertainty exists in ZET market shares, due 
to technological, operational, and cost variations.”  
Cost of charging
The charging costs hinge on charging infrastructure delivery 
mechanisms and local electricity tariffs. In this study, charging 
costs vary by type of charging station, including depot or NCS 
stations, DCFC stations, and MCS stations, due to their different 
investment scales and time of charging. 
Depot or NCS cost: Because the cost of overnight charging 
is relatively lower, this study assumes that the depot or NCS 
cost is 0.9 CNY/kWh (including utility tariffs and charging 
infrastructure investments), based on Pers. Comm. 2024. With 
the improved availability of depot or NCS stations at both ends 
of trips and the ability to maximize charging during off-peak 
times, this study assumes that the depot or NCS cost in the 
Enhanced policy scenario would reduce to 0.6 CNY/kWh 
(Pers. Comm. 2024).
DCFC cost: The average charging cost of 1.2 CNY/kWh (includ -
ing utility tariffs and charging infrastructure investments) is 
obtained and validated by Chen and Xue 2024 and Pers. Comm. 
2024. This average cost is used to simulate ZETs’ TCO from 
MY2024 to MY2035. 18 
MCS cost: Due to the lack of technology breakthrough and 
unified charging standards for MCS charging (China SAE 2024), 
the surcharge on MCS chargers to recoup the infrastructure 
investments is assumed to be 1.2 CNY/kWh in 2024. As the MCS 
technology ramps up, this surcharge will reduce sharply to 0.7 
CNY/kWh in 2030 and 0.4 CNY/kWh in 2035 (Zhu et al. 2023; 
Goeckeler et al. 2023). This study assumes that truck drivers will 
avoid charging at peak hours; therefore, the underlying utility 
tariff is set to 0.9 CNY/kWh (Chen and Xue 2024). As a result, 
the MCS charging cost will reduce from 2.1 CNY/kWh in 2024 to 
1.6 CNY/kWh in 2030 and 1.3 CNY/kWh in 2035. 
At-pump hydrogen prices
The study assumes that investments in hydrogen refueling 
infrastructure and other relevant infrastructure are recouped by 
at-pump hydrogen prices.
The 2024 at-pump hydrogen price was based on the average 
value from China’s Hydrogen Price Index (China Hydrogen Alli-
ance 2025). The future hydrogen costs (2025–35) are projected 
by WRI authors by considering hydrogen supply mix (Sinopec 
2023) and estimated at-pump costs for green hydrogen (Yuan 
et al. 2023; Sun et al. 2023) and gray hydrogen (Sinopec 2023). 
Currently, gray hydrogen dominates China’s hydrogen supply. 
However, by 2035, green hydrogen is expected to account 
for 24 percent of the hydrogen supply (Sinopec 2023), with a 
projected at-pump cost of 28 CNY/kg (Yuan et al. 2023; Sun et 
al. 2023), if assuming the high utilization of hydrogen refueling 
stations and the adoption of liquid hydrogen technologies 
(transported via tank trucks) or pipelines for long-distance 
transportation of hydrogen.
Expressway tolls 
Road charge is calculated based on expressway toll rates and 
percentage of AVKT on expressways. The expressway toll rates 
are the national average: 205 CNY/100 km for tractor trailers 
and 116 CNY/100 km for straight trucks (NDRC 2024). The per -
centage of AVKT on expressways is based on AQSIQ and SAC 
2011 and China SAE n.d., where the ratio is set to 50 percent 
and 90 percent for the RD and LH applications, respectively.

ENDNOTES
1. Closed-loop applications are operations conducted within 
confined industrial settings—such as ports, mines, steel mills, 
or industrial parks—that typically involve short-distance, 
high-frequency trips.
2. In this study, the terms “truck owners” and “truck [or “fleet”] 
operators” are used interchangeably.
3. High time-sensitivity operations are transportation activi -
ties with strict delivery time requirements, such as express 
parcel delivery.
4. The CO2 emission data were for the year 2020 due to a lack 
of recent statistics.  
5. In 2024, the Ministry of Transport and the Ministry of Finance 
introduced a trade-in policy that provides subsidies for 
scrapping diesel trucks meeting China III or lower emission 
standards and/or purchasing new energy trucks. Start -
ing in 2025, the scrappage subsidy also extends to used 
China IV trucks (MOT et al. 2025; MOT and MOF 2024). This 
study assumes that these trade-in subsidies will continue 
through 2027, aligning with the timeframe of “Action Plan for 
Promoting Large-Scale Equipment Renewal and Trade-In of 
Consumer Goods” and local trade-in policies (State Council 
2024; Shanghai Government 2024). 
6. The national annual average income is the average wage 
of employed people in urban units in the national statistical 
yearbooks. 
7. “NCS” refers to public or semipublic charging stations that 
provide overnight charging for trucks. NCS typically have 
higher charging costs than privately owned depots.
8. Notably, this study projects ZET market share by application. 
Due to lack of statistics on truck sales by application, we are 
unable estimate China’s overall ZET market share. 
9. Since 2019, the MEE introduced a performance rating 
scheme for high-emission industries in which companies are 
classified as level A, B, or C based on their adoption of zero-
emission heavy-duty trucks or railway or waterway trans -
portation. Companies rated as level A can operate as usual 
on heavily polluted days. Level C companies should reduce 
operation by half or even suspend production on heavily pol -
luted days (MEE 2020).
10. This finding differs from the RD application because in the 
LH application BETs do not have short payback periods 
until MY2034–35 and the costs of FCETs drop rapidly after 
MY2030, whereas in the RD application, BETs are already 
cost-competitive by and around MY2030.
11. For the case of shorter DVKT (300 km), this study reduces 
the energy storage capacities for longer-range ZETs to fit the 
DVKT need, such as from BET400 and FCET400 to BET300 
and FCET300, whereas for shorter-range BETs, the battery 
capacities remain the same (that is, BET200_DCFC and 
BET200_MCS).
12. This finding applies specifically to tractor trailers and may 
not hold for straight trucks, given their greater payload 
losses.
13. For the case of longer DVKT (1,000 km), this study increases 
the energy storage capacities for both shorter- and longer-
range ZETs to fit the DVKT need, updating from BET400 to 
BET500, BET800 to BET1000, and FCET800 to FCET1000.
14. This study does not consider the subsidy for replacing a 
used truck with a China VI diesel truck, since fleet operators 
would prioritize switching to new energy trucks due to their 
lower operational costs (Pers. Comm. 2024). This approach 
would not affect the estimated ZET market share in the Cur -
rent policy scenario.
15. The decline in market share by MY2030 is attributed to the 
phaseout of the current policies.
16. The costs are assumed to be 486 CNY/kW for the OBC and 
389 CNY/kW for the DC/DC converter. These costs are as -
sumed to be constant over time.
17. The average of diesel prices over the past decade (2015–24) 
is even lower, reaching 6.7 CNY/L (Eastmoney 2022).
18. This means that for the case without policy incentives, de -
mand charges are also waived for ZETs.

ABOUT WRI
World Resources Institute is a global research organization 
that turns big ideas into action at the nexus of environment, 
economic opportunity, and human well-being. 
Our Challenge 
Natural resources are at the foundation of economic oppor -
tunity and human well-being. But today, we are depleting 
Earth’s resources at rates that are not sustainable, endangering 
economies and people’s lives. People depend on clean water, 
fertile land, healthy forests, and a stable climate. Livable cities 
and clean energy are essential for a sustainable planet. We 
must address these urgent, global challenges this decade. 
Our Vision 
We envision an equitable and prosperous planet driven by the 
wise management of natural resources. We aspire to create a 
world where the actions of government, business, and com -
munities combine to eliminate poverty and sustain the natural 
environment for all people. 
Our Approach 
COUNT IT 
We start with data. We conduct independent research and 
draw on the latest technology to develop new insights and 
recommendations. Our rigorous analysis identifies risks, unveils 
opportunities, and informs smart strategies. We focus our 
efforts on influential and emerging economies where the future 
of sustainability will be determined. 
CHANGE IT 
We use our research to influence government policies, busi -
ness strategies, and civil society action. We test projects with 
communities, companies, and government agencies to build a 
strong evidence base. Then, we work with partners to deliver 
change on the ground that alleviates poverty and strengthens 
society. We hold ourselves accountable to ensure our outcomes 
will be bold and enduring. 
SCALE IT 
We don’t think small. Once tested, we work with partners 
to adopt and expand our efforts regionally and globally. We 
engage with decision-makers to carry out our ideas and elevate 
our impact. We measure success through government and 
business actions that improve people’s lives and sustain a 
healthy environment.
